Eventide Compact

A history of the refusal

Frontier record / Origins of EVPACT

The Eventide Compact began with a failed search for a place to belong. Captain Korvyr approached a large interstellar trading corporation expecting the useful things an association could offer: shared work, dependable crews, and strength enough to make an honest living beyond the reach of reliable help.

What he found instead would become the reason for founding something else.

The corporation before the Compact

Within the trading corporation, authority had become an end in itself. Overzealous officers treated scrutiny as disloyalty. Corruption and infighting turned cooperation into a contest over influence, while conduct that would have been called piracy outside the corporation was justified when it served those inside it.

The objection was never to organization itself. Crews need coordination. Cargo needs accounting. Someone has to call the departure. The failure came when authority stopped answering for its decisions and began treating its own advantage as sufficient cause.

A corporate name could make a requisition sound respectable. It could not make the taking honest.

Reedrick leaves. Korvyr follows.

Captain Reedrick, a close friend of Korvyr's, left first. Korvyr followed soon after. Their departures were separate decisions with a common conclusion: remaining under that authority would mean accepting conduct they could no longer defend.

Korvyr established the Eventide Compact as a way for independent captains and crews to stand together against megacorporate coercion, unjust authority, and pirates, including those who practiced piracy beneath a respectable name.

Reedrick stands beside him among the Compact's First Signatories and Convenors. Their names appear on the charter as obligations accepted, with the same scrutiny due to any other captain.

The agreement they chose to keep

The Compact's answer was practical. Share work. Agree the shares. Carry freight, recover the stranded, repair the damaged, and provide protection when a lone crew would have little meaningful choice. Keep the people doing the work independent enough to benefit from it.

That purpose reaches across claimed and lawless space. Legitimate ownership and applicable laws remain obligations. Assertions of jurisdiction, entitlement, and authority remain open to examination, including those made by the Compact itself.

Leaving an abusive institution would mean little if its habits followed aboard. Mutual protection therefore carries accountability: a member's distress deserves attention; their account still requires evidence. The pact cannot become cover for a private shakedown.

A reputation still being written

Some outsiders call the Compact's members pirates. A refusal is easily retold by the person whose demand was refused. In the Compact's own record, it bears no official ill-standing with the UEE or another recognized governing body; that is part of its founding account, not a claim that every dispute has already been settled in its favor.

An unsupported requisition may meet a calm refusal. The captain will give a name, preserve the evidence, and sign the report. The person making the demand can do the same.

The Eventide Compact is still young. Its history is a point of departure, and the test of what follows is whether its crews keep the agreement when keeping it costs them something.

Our word binds us.
